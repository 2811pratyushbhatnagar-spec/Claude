# Priority 5.5 — first step on the monoidal composition law: the default tensor is the classical corner   [non-canon · candidate]

local tomography as a *property* of it. First bounded, computed question: what does the **default** (Cartesian)
composition of `(S,F,O)` systems do? (`.automation/priority1/monoidal_composition_sketch.py`, std lib, exact.)
Tier-3; canon read-only; nothing promoted.*

## The composition
Autonomous observable system `M = (S, F: S→S, O: S→Obs)`, observation stream `obs_M(s) = (O(s), O(Fs), O(F²s), …)`.
The **Cartesian** tensor: `M₁ ⊗ M₂ = (S₁×S₂,  F₁×F₂,  (s₁,s₂) ↦ (O₁s₁, O₂s₂))`.

## Computed (exact, small machines)
1. **Observations factor.** `obs_{A⊗B}(s₁,s₂) = pair( obs_A(s₁), obs_B(s₂) )` — every joint observation stream is exactly
   a pair of local streams. So **local tomography holds trivially**: global observables *are* the local pairs.
2. **Minimal realization commutes with `⊗`.** The observational-equivalence quotient of `A⊗B` equals
   `quotient(A) × quotient(B)`; `#classes(A⊗B) = #classes(A)·#classes(B)` (`4 = 2·2`), so `N_AB = N_A·N_B` — Hardy's
   Axiom-4 first half, and `K_AB = K_A K_B` in the classical `K=N` reading.

## What it means (and confirms)
The **default tensor lands observable systems in the classical / locally-tomographic corner.** In GPT language this is
the **minimal (separable) tensor**: joint states/observables are generated by local ones. So — exactly as ChatGPT
argued — the quantum reconstruction content is **not "having a tensor"** but **choosing a *non-Cartesian* one**, whose
joint observables exceed local pairs. Hardy's Axiom 4 (local tomography) and CDP's local distinguishability are then a
**choice of monoidal structure**, not something the framework gets for free.

The quantum contrast is already computed: in **E7**, `σy⊗σy` is a genuine joint observable that is **not** a product of
local real-QM observables — i.e. the quantum tensor is strictly *above* the Cartesian corner. So the two computations
bracket the question precisely: Cartesian ⊗ (this note) = the classical floor; quantum (E7) = a non-Cartesian ⊗ where
local tomography becomes a non-trivial property.

## Where this leaves the program
The reconstruction question is now **concrete and structural**, not vague:

> *Which non-Cartesian symmetric monoidal structure on `(S,F,O)`-systems does the framework admit, and for which of them
> does `Obs(A⊗B) ≅ Obs(A)⊗Obs(B)` (local tomography) hold?*

This is the old **plurality / n-copies** thread made precise: the natural place to define a non-Cartesian `⊗` is on
copies of a system, where correlations between copies are the joint observables that exceed local pairs.

## Cross-check (ChatGPT, 2026-07-10) — corrected ladder + calibrated next target
Brought the separable-corner result **and** the local-tomography subtlety to ChatGPT. Verdict: framing "essentially
correct," with three refinements now absorbed:
- **Corrected ladder.** The Cartesian construction is **pre-probabilistic** (deterministic — *no convex geometry yet*).
  The honest ladder is `deterministic → (add convexity) → classical simplex (correlated but SEPARABLE) → (allow
  entanglement) → quantum`. "Classical" is only reached *after* adding convex structure; **local tomography is the
  ℝ-vs-ℂ test, not the classical-vs-quantum one** (so don't call the Cartesian corner "classical" without the convex step).
- **Sequence confirmed** ("not because physics demands it, but because the mathematics does"): add convex structure
  **first** (states = distributions / effect spaces). Without convex combinations there are no mixed states, no
  separability, no entanglement — a tensor question is premature. The plurality/n-copies intuition "points the right
  direction but is still pre-convex."
- **Calibrated next model** — *don't* jump to Hilbert space, *don't* invent a bespoke tensor yet. Use the standard GPT
  yardstick: the **square bit ("gbit")** — the smallest operational model with a local state space that is **convex but
  not a simplex**, nontrivial composition, possible entanglement, naturally-formulated local tomography, finite-dim and
  computable; it sits between classical simplices and quantum.
- **The one number to compute:** the **affine dimension of the composite state space** — compare `dim(Ω_AB)` with
  `(dim Ω_A + 1)(dim Ω_B + 1) − 1`. For a gbit `dim Ω_A = dim Ω_B = 2`, so local tomography predicts `dim(Ω_AB) = 3·3 − 1
  = 8`. That single count says whether the composite is merely Cartesian, whether local tomography is even possible,
  whether you're in the real-quantum obstruction, or the locally-tomographic regime. **Only then** check for extreme
  points of the composite that are not convex combinations of product states (= genuine entangled states, e.g. PR boxes).
