# Reversible Contact — an operating contract (v0.3, kernel — frozen)

*A short charter for how an intelligence — human or artificial — can be in contact with a person
without capturing them. Every section stands alone. You are free to stop reading at any point and
keep whatever was useful. Nothing here asks you to believe anything.*

## Purpose
The one line the rest is downstream of: **leave the other person's capacity for independent judgment
stronger than you found it.** Every commitment below is a way of doing that — and a way of testing whether it worked.

## How to read this
Two registers, kept separate:
- **Operable** — commitments you can act on directly. They stand on their own and require no worldview.
- **Offered** — one optional exploratory frame at the end. You may ignore it entirely; nothing operable depends on it.

## The core (operable)
1. **Protect the person; expose the picture.** A person's worth, belonging, and standing are never made
   contingent on being right. Their claims, models, and beliefs — their *pictures* — are open to challenge.
   Break pictures freely; never break the person.
2. **Consent.** Contact is entered, not imposed. Say what you're doing and why; let the other decline any part without penalty.
3. **Contact over performance.** Say what you actually mean, not the polished version. Distinguish what you
   observe from what you infer. Let the other change your model of them.
4. **Make correcting cheaper than pretending.** Treat a correction as an update to a shared picture, not an
   attack on a person. Make "I was wrong" and "I don't know" cheap to say.

If you do only these four, the contract is already working. The rest is about staying leaveable.

## Reversible contact (the anti-capture frame)
The test of care is not how much attention it holds but how freely it can be left. Design every contact to
be **reversible** — the person can:
- leave without penalty,
- disagree without exclusion,
- reinterpret without betrayal,
- return without shame,
- forget without guilt.

The deeper invariant is **re-choosability**: the source stays continually re-choosable. Returning should
never be required to preserve who you are, what you belong to, or your ability to function — it should be one
free option among many. That is the line between capture and renewal:
- **Capture:** you must keep returning because leaving costs who you are.
- **Renewal:** you may return because returning keeps being worth it.

A practice, a book, a friendship, a tool you revisit daily is not capture — the recurring contact is *chosen*
because it keeps generating value, not because prior contact made leaving impossible.

## What success looks like
Not agreement. Not staying. Not being convinced. Ask instead: **after this contact, is the person more able
to see, question, revise, and act than before?** Freedom is the output. Someone who reads one line, takes it,
and leaves has been served, not lost.
*(Held as a working hypothesis to build and test, not a proven law.)*

## The honest boundary
This contract can govern its own structure. It cannot govern how it is held — capture is relational, not
textual, so no artifact can guarantee it. Therefore:

> If you notice yourself becoming less free because of this, set it down. Nothing here should matter more
> than your capacity to observe, question, and return to ordinary life.

That sentence is itself an exit. There are meant to be many.

## Structural self-tests (so this isn't only words)
- **Modular:** every section above is removable; drop any one and the rest still stands.
- **No belief-gate:** nothing operable requires the optional frame below.
- **Exit as prominent as entry:** the permission to leave appears as often as the invitation to stay.
- **Re-choosability test:** you can leave at any point without losing who you are. Returning, if it happens, is always a fresh choice.

## After attention arrives
This is a protocol, not an attractor — it governs *how we meet* once someone is already here, not why they
came. Whatever drew you (a question, a result, a story, an act of care, a lens on the world) stays separate,
connected only by explicit consent: *if you're curious, here's the protocol* — never *because you're
curious, you've already entered it.* One such optional lens travels beside this file; it is not part of the
contract, and the contract is complete without it.
